## 1 Scope

Applies to any confirmed or suspected combustion event in the production hall, including
electrical fires in machine cabinets. Covers detection thresholds, the suppression sequence and
the conditions under which suppression may be discharged.

## 2 Declaring a fire

2.1 Smoke thresholds for optical particulate sensors: **20 ppm warning**, **40 ppm critical**.

2.2 A fire is declared only on **two corroborating signals**. Acceptable combinations:

- smoke at or above the critical level **and** the reading persisting across at least three
  consecutive samples (alarm verification);
- smoke at or above the critical level **and** a rising smoke trend;
- smoke present **and** ambient temperature at or above 50 °C, or rising faster than
  4 °C per minute.

2.3 **Smoke must be present.** Temperature alone never declares a fire, however high. A hot
machine with no combustion signature is a mechanical fault — see SOP-M04 §4.2. Discharging
suppression on it is prohibited by SOP-M04 §5.1.

2.4 A single sample above the critical level is *not* a fire. Optical sensors false-trigger on
welding fume, coolant mist and dust. Wait for verification.

## 3 Suppression sequence

### 3.1 Immediately and automatically

3.1.1 Sound the acoustic and strobe evacuation alarm for the affected zone. This is a low-risk
action and does not need authorisation — the cost of a false alarm is far below the cost of a
delayed evacuation.

### 3.2 On the owner's authorisation

3.2.1 Close the fire containment doors for the zone to seal ventilation dampers and stop aerosol
propagation into neighbouring sectors.
3.2.2 De-energise the 400 V supply lines feeding the affected sector.
3.2.3 **Discharge clean-agent suppression.** This is the action that removes the hazard.

### 3.3 Order of operations

3.3.1 Personnel first, containment second, suppression third. Never discharge before the zone
reports clear — see EVAC-PROC §2.
3.3.2 Allow the documented 30-second pre-discharge delay to run. Do not shorten it.

## 4 Verification before closing the incident

4.1 Smoke back below the 20 ppm warning level and falling.
4.2 Ambient temperature falling.
4.3 Zero personnel inside the zone.
4.4 Containment doors confirmed closed by their limit switches.

## 5 After the event

5.1 Do not re-enter for 10 minutes after a clean-agent discharge; the agent displaces oxygen.
5.2 Preserve the sensor history for the incident review — the trend that triggered the alarm is
the primary evidence.
5.3 A zone that has just been cleared must not re-trigger on residual smoke. Only a **rising**
reading indicates a new or re-ignited fire.
